title: "The Role I Never Auditioned For"
date: 2026-05-14
description: "Every family has its stories. In mine, I was the problem. This is where I start to tell a different version."
---

Every family has its mythology. The golden child. The peacekeeper. The one who got away. And then there's the scapegoat — the one who carries everyone else's discomfort so they don't have to feel it themselves.

I didn't know I had a role until I tried to stop playing it.

---

For years, I thought the problem was me. I was "too sensitive," "too dramatic," "too much." These weren't observations — they were assignments. They were the family's way of saying: *we need someone to blame, and you've been volunteered.*

The scapegoat doesn't get cast through a formal process. There's no meeting, no vote. It happens slowly, the way erosion happens — through a thousand small moments of redirection. A parent's frustration lands on the child who reacts. A sibling's cruelty gets reframed as "teasing." A pattern sets in, and before long, everyone knows their part.

> "The scapegoat is the truth-teller in a family that has agreed to lie."

I don't know who said that first, but it found me at the right time.

---

This is not a blog about revenge. It's not about exposing anyone by name or settling scores. It's about telling the truth — *my* truth — in a space where no one can interrupt me, correct me, or tell me I'm remembering it wrong.

It's about the slow work of separating who I was told I am from who I actually am.

If you've ever been the "difficult" one, the one who asked the questions nobody wanted asked, the one whose emotions were treated as evidence of instability rather than a reasonable response to an unreasonable situation — these essays are for you.

Welcome. Pull up a chair. You're not too much here.
